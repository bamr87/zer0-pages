# Timeline of 1987


A chronological index of notable events from the year 1987. Each entry links to its topic article.


| Date | Category | Event |
|------|----------|-------|
| Jan 21 | Arts & Culture | **[[_kb/1987/arts-culture/aretha-franklin-hof|Aretha Franklin]]** becomes the first woman inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame, at the ceremony at the Waldorf-Astoria Hotel in New York City. |
| Feb 23 | Science & Technology | **[[_kb/1987/science-technology/supernova-1987a|Supernova 1987A]]** is observed in the Large Magellanic Cloud — the brightest supernova visible from Earth since 1604 — providing unprecedented data on stellar death, including the first detection of supernova neutrinos. |
| Mar 9 | Arts & Culture | **[[_kb/1987/arts-culture/the-joshua-tree|The Joshua Tree released]]** — U2 release their fifth studio album, which becomes Britain's fastest-selling record (235,000 copies in one week), reaches number one in over 20 countries, and wins the 1988 Grammy Album of the Year. |
| Mar 12 | Society & Economics | **[[_kb/1987/society-economics/act-up-founding|ACT UP founded]]** — the AIDS Coalition to Unleash Power is established at the Lesbian and Gay Community Services Center in New York City by ~300 activists, launching a direct-action movement that transforms U.S. AIDS policy and FDA drug-approval processes. |
| Mar 20 | Science & Technology | **[[_kb/1987/science-technology/azt-fda-approval|FDA approves AZT]]** — the FDA approves zidovudine (Retrovir), the first antiretroviral drug for HIV/AIDS, in a record-fast 25-month development cycle; the approval is the fastest in FDA history to that point. |
| Mar 31 | Arts & Culture | **[[_kb/1987/arts-culture/sign-o-the-times|Sign 'O' the Times released]]** — Prince releases his ninth studio album as a double LP, widely considered his finest work and one of the greatest albums in popular music history; the title track addresses AIDS, gang violence, and drug abuse. |
| Apr 2 | Science & Technology | **[[_kb/1987/science-technology/ibm-ps2|IBM PS/2 launched]]** — IBM introduces the Personal System/2 line, standardizing VGA graphics, PS/2 keyboard/mouse ports, and 3.5-inch floppies; its proprietary MCA bus is rejected by clone makers who form the EISA consortium, effectively ending IBM's control of the PC market. |
| Apr 5 | Society & Economics | **[[_kb/1987/society-economics/fox-broadcasting|Fox Broadcasting Company launches primetime]]** — Rupert Murdoch's Fox debuts *Married...with Children* and *The Tracey Ullman Show*, becoming the first successful fourth U.S. broadcast network since DuMont ended in 1956 and targeting the 18–34 demographic underserved by the Big Three networks. |
| May 8 | People | **[[_kb/1987/people/gary-hart|Gary Hart suspends presidential campaign]]** — the leading Democratic frontrunner withdraws after the Miami Herald reports his relationship with model Donna Rice, permanently transforming the media's scrutiny of candidates' private lives. |
| May 28 | People | **[[_kb/1987/people/mathias-rust|Mathias Rust lands in Red Square]]** — an 18-year-old West German amateur pilot flies a Cessna from Helsinki to Moscow and lands beside the Kremlin, exposing Soviet air-defense failures and triggering the largest Soviet military purge since the 1930s. |
| Jun 11 | People | **[[_kb/1987/people/margaret-thatcher|Margaret Thatcher wins third general election]]** — the Conservative Party wins 376 seats with a 102-seat majority; Thatcher becomes the first British Prime Minister since 1820 to win three consecutive elections and the only PM to do so in the twentieth century. |
| Jun 12 | History & Politics | **[[_kb/1987/history-politics/reagan-berlin-speech|"Tear Down This Wall" speech]]** — President Reagan challenges Gorbachev at the Brandenburg Gate in West Berlin before ~45,000 people; the phrase becomes a defining symbol of Cold War resolution. |
| Jun 26 | Arts & Culture | **[[_kb/1987/arts-culture/full-metal-jacket|Full Metal Jacket]]** — Stanley Kubrick's two-part Vietnam War film premieres, joining *RoboCop* and *Lethal Weapon* in a landmark year for Hollywood. |
| Jul 4 | People | **[[_kb/1987/people/klaus-barbie|Klaus Barbie convicted]]** — former Lyon Gestapo chief is found guilty in France's first crimes-against-humanity prosecution and sentenced to life imprisonment for the deportation and murder of thousands, including 44 children from the Izieu home. |
| Jul 7 | History & Politics | **[[_kb/1987/history-politics/iran-contra|Iran-Contra hearings]]** — Oliver North begins six days of nationally televised testimony before the joint congressional committee, drawing 55 million viewers and making the arms-for-hostages scandal a defining moment of Reagan's presidency. |
| Aug 17 | People | **[[_kb/1987/people/rudolf-hess|Rudolf Hess]]**, Hitler's former deputy, dies at 93 in Spandau Prison, Berlin — its sole prisoner since 1966 — ending four-power Allied custody and prompting the prison's demolition. |
| Aug 31 | Arts & Culture | **[[_kb/1987/arts-culture/michael-jackson-bad|Michael Jackson's *Bad* released]]** — Jackson's seventh studio album becomes the first in history to produce five Billboard Hot 100 number-one singles; the accompanying Bad World Tour draws 4.4 million attendees across 15 countries. |
| Sep 16 | Arts & Culture | **[[_kb/1987/arts-culture/beloved|*Beloved* by Toni Morrison published]]** — Morrison's fifth novel, based on the true story of Margaret Garner, wins the 1988 Pulitzer Prize for Fiction and is later ranked by a *New York Times* survey as the best American fiction published between 1981 and 2006. |
| Sep 16 | History & Politics | **[[_kb/1987/history-politics/montreal-protocol|Montreal Protocol]]** is signed by 46 nations, the first global environmental protection agreement, aimed at phasing out ozone-depleting CFCs. |
| Oct 17 | Society & Economics | **[[_kb/1987/society-economics/world-series-1987|1987 World Series begins]]** — the Minnesota Twins face the St. Louis Cardinals in the first World Series played entirely indoors; the Twins win all four home games and clinch the championship on October 25. |
| Oct 19 | Society & Economics | **[[_kb/1987/society-economics/black-monday|Black Monday]]** — the Dow Jones Industrial Average falls 22.6% in a single day, the largest one-day percentage drop in history; worldwide losses estimated at US$1.71 trillion. |
| Nov 13 | Science & Technology | **[[_kb/1987/science-technology/dna-fingerprinting|DNA fingerprinting]]** — Robert Melias (UK) becomes the first person in the world convicted using DNA evidence; Tommie Lee Andrews (USA) is convicted by the same method the same month. |
| Dec 8 | History & Politics | **[[_kb/1987/history-politics/inf-treaty|INF Treaty signed]]** — Reagan and Gorbachev sign the Intermediate-Range Nuclear Forces Treaty in Washington, the first arms-control agreement to eliminate an entire class of nuclear weapons. |
| Dec 9 | History & Politics | **[[_kb/1987/history-politics/first-intifada|First Intifada begins]]** — a Palestinian uprising against Israeli occupation erupts in Gaza and the West Bank following a fatal crash at Erez checkpoint, launching a six-year struggle that leads to the Oslo Accords. |
| Dec 9 | Science & Technology | **[[_kb/1987/science-technology/windows-2|Microsoft Windows 2.0 released]]** — Microsoft launches Windows 2.0 with overlapping windows, desktop icons, and a Windows/386 protected-mode variant, triggering the landmark Apple v. Microsoft "look and feel" lawsuit. |
| Dec 29 | Society & Economics | **[[_kb/1987/society-economics/prozac-fda-approval|FDA approves Prozac]]** — fluoxetine becomes the first SSRI approved in the United States, transforming treatment of depression and launching the SSRI era in psychiatry. |
